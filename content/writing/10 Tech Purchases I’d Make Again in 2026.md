# 10 Tech Purchases I’d Make Again in 2026

[10 Tech Purchases I’d Make Again in 2026](https://youtu.be/fIGMPlkHJ44)

A lot of tech favorites videos cover the same ground: monitors, headphones, keyboards, the products where you could probably find five videos comparing all the specs. I wanted to share the things I touch every single day, or at least multiple times a week. These are intentional, well-designed products that have made my life better, and that I’d recommend to a friend or family member. I want more well-designed, intentional products and less “stuff” that’s overhyped or won’t last.

This is in no particular order, and none of these products are sponsored. The links go to Amazon.

## 1. Kindle

[Kindle on Amazon](https://amzn.to/4rZgKIo)

I love that this product does one thing very well, and that’s read. It’s much better on your eyes than staring at your phone or an iPad. I typically spend 45 minutes to an hour reading every night, and my partner is often asleep before me, so being able to keep reading without a device that lights up the whole room is a plus. If you want to read more or get off your phone at night, just get one.

My only caveat is that if you’re buying new, I’d get one of the newer models that lets you adjust the temperature of the display and not just the brightness. I tested this in store and could definitely notice a difference, especially at night when it’s the only light on. That said, any Kindle is probably fine if you’re just looking to start reading.

## 2. Philips Sunrise Lamp

[Sunrise lamp on Amazon](https://amzn.to/3MRNcgR)

About a year ago my family and I went to the beach and stayed in an Airbnb. My room had east-facing windows, and there was something about waking up to natural golden, warm light in the morning, especially in the summer, that made such a difference. In our bedroom here we have blackout curtains, because there are tons of street lights and other ambient light that would otherwise keep us up all night. So ironically we have blackout curtains, and then we simulate a sunrise with this Philips light.

The reason I love it is that you can set a really long wake-up window, up to about 45 minutes I think, where it gradually gets brighter and brighter. My alarm goes off at 8, so I have it start warming up at 7:15, and by the time it’s at full brightness the alarm is going off. It’s a more natural way to get out of bed.

I’ve gone a step further and rotated it to face into a wall. Fortunately we have white walls, so the light bounces really nicely. It’s not even shining on us, it fills a whole corner of the room, and since it’s a shared wall with a window, it tricks your brain into thinking it’s real light. I could understand this sounding gimmicky if you get a ton of natural light, but in the winter it made a huge difference for us.

## 3. Dreame Vacuum Mop

[Vacuum mop on Amazon](https://amzn.to/47zP0ls)

I’d been getting a ton of Dreame ads on TikTok for a while, so I finally pulled the trigger on the H12 Pro. It has a really long run time, and it bends pretty low so you can get underneath furniture. The main thing I love is that it gets really close to the edge of wherever you’re going, right up against your cabinets and walls and into tight corners, so it cleans the edges and not just the center.

It also has a self-cleaning and drying mode. When you put it in the dock to charge, you can have it run a clean cycle and then dry itself. When I was researching other hardwood cleaners, that was something a lot of them didn’t have. You end up cleaning off all that dirt, but then the wet roller just sits there, collects it over a couple of days and gets a funky smell. I’ll admit I’ve been using this almost every single day. It’s a really nice way to reset at the end of the day: when you’re finished cleaning the kitchen, do a quick mop. We also have cats, so being able to clean around their litter box has been really nice. If you live with other people or have pets, it’s close to a must-have.

## 4. iPad Mini

[iPad Mini on Amazon](https://amzn.to/4d7Vvj0)

This is kind of a full circle purchase for me. I owned the original iPad Mini when it came out 15 years ago, and I’ve always thought it’s the perfect size. It’s bigger than your phone, so it’s nice to have the extra real estate, but it never feels heavy or bulky like carrying around the screen of a laptop, which is how I feel about the iPad Pros and other big tablets.

I’m also trying to stay off my phone, using it more as a productivity tool and a utility. In between, I mostly use the iPad Mini for entertainment: a ton of YouTube and TV shows, and social media, specifically on the iPad. I don’t have any social apps installed on my phone, so I can limit my time on those platforms. Besides that, I use it for quick sketches for work, and right now as a preview monitor plugged into my camera. It’s perfect on a plane, too. I have a small Lululemon bag that fits my iPad, my passport and some AirPods, which is the perfect compact travel kit I’ve found.

My main gripe is the screen, and not even the quality, just the refresh rate. When you’re scrolling a web page and reading text you can see some jittering, what they call jelly scrolling. I think it’s silly that in 2026 we’re still only getting 60 Hz. The iPad Airs and Pros are noticeably better, but I’m sure it’s a trade-off for keeping the price low. If you’re just watching shows and hanging out on it, it’s a great device.

## 5. Monitor arm

[Monitor arm on Amazon](https://amzn.to/4aXcwuZ)

This one’s not really a piece of tech, but it supports my biggest piece of tech. About six months ago I upgraded to an ultrawide monitor, and the stands that come with these are typically really poor quality. Every time I bump the desk or type, it rattles. Investing in a solid monitor arm that can support the weight, and that you can get exactly where you need, makes such a big difference. I also have a desk shelf, and I love how the monitor looks like it’s floating right above it. I can fit my laptops underneath the screen and still have space for storage, without the clutter of the massive stands.

The one I bought is around $100, and I’d honestly recommend not going below that unless you find a good deal. Most of the budget options work for a couple of weeks or maybe a few months, and then they slowly start to loosen, or something breaks. If you’re investing in a really good monitor, it’s not worth cheaping out on the stand. Set it once and forget it.

## 6. iPhone 17 Pro, but probably not for the reason you think

[iPhone 17 Pro battery on Amazon](https://amzn.to/4cA1EV9)

I upgraded from the 15 Pro, which in hindsight was probably unnecessary, but I was really looking forward to the rear cameras, the way they were advertised with Apple ProRAW and the zoom lens. At the time I hadn’t invested in good lenses yet, and I was considering using my iPhone as my daily camera.

What actually shocked me was the front-facing camera and the battery. A bigger sensor for the front camera, one that automatically resizes depending on who’s in frame with you, makes such a difference for group selfies. Definitely a first world problem, but I was shocked at how good it is. The second thing was the battery life. I’m not an intense phone user by any means, but I have it with me throughout the day checking Slack messages, and watching videos for work (people send me Loom videos all the time). It doesn’t just sit in my pocket, and I rarely get below 50 percent by the end of a given day. On long travel days that’s even better, because I’m never worried about having enough to call an Uber or pull up Maps.

## 7. A good tripod

[K&F Concept tripod on Amazon](https://amzn.to/3P8idO2)

I was given the K&F Concept tripod recently, and this thing is a beast. It gets incredibly tall, the tallest I’ve ever seen a tripod get, so you can use it for pretty much any angle. The arm can also rotate 90 degrees, so you can get a perfect top-down shot without showing the tripod. I’ve used it with my phone mounted on the end, and with the camera I’m shooting on now. Right now I’m also using it to hold a softbox, and for B-roll around the apartment.

It’s really well built: quick release, smooth adjustments, nothing feels cheap. It collapses small enough for a camera bag. I’m actually considering the tabletop version too, because right now I have a Joby tripod combined with a podcast mic stand and it’s not the greatest setup. Being able to use the same ecosystem of adapters and mounts would be awesome. I don’t think they’re super expensive, but I’d skip anything under $100 and invest here, because the quality gap is real.

## 8. Sonos Beam soundbar

[Sonos soundbar on Amazon](https://amzn.to/4sxC9bw)

For the last five or six years I had a run-of-the-mill Samsung soundbar, a 2.1 setup with a subwoofer, but for the last two years, every 10 minutes or so, the sub would cut out for about five to seven seconds. I finally just wanted something that worked reliably and played nicely with our Apple TV.

A couple of things I love about the Sonos. One, it’s built really well. When you take it out of the box you can feel it’s a hefty product, not so heavy that I was worried about putting it on our TV stand, but it feels well built. Setup was really easy, and while there are settings to adjust the EQ, I didn’t feel the need to. I trusted it was going to sound good right off the bat. Two, they have a really solid ecosystem, so you can build your own modular home theater. We’re in an apartment, so I’m not going to go crazy, but knowing that if we move somewhere with more space I can keep adding speakers without starting from scratch is a really nice plus.

One callout: if you’re coming from any setup with a subwoofer to just a soundbar, you will notice the lack of low end. That’s not to say the Sonos doesn’t sound great all around. I’m sure that if I’d turned off the sub in the Samsung setup, which was happening anyway every 10 minutes, it would have sounded a lot thinner than this does. And knowing that I can expand down the road is a nice plus.

## 9. Oura Ring

[Oura Ring on Amazon](https://amzn.to/3Pb3ou9)

I’ve been an Oura user for about a year now, and I’ve probably never taken it off for longer than a couple of hours to charge, so almost every single day for over a year. The core value is that it’s a passive health tracker. If you don’t want notifications, you don’t want to feel like you’re always tracking something, and you don’t want it to be invasive, this is the route to take. There’s no screen you’re always looking at. It’s a lot more incognito than an Apple Watch.

It’s an incredible sleep tracker, and it’s good at tracking your stress levels and your recovery time. My wife and I both have one, and multiple times in the last year it’s predicted that we were getting sick before we even felt sick. That’s how good the stress tracking is.

The two limitations I ran into: it’s not great at fitness tracking. You can’t take it into the gym and lift, since there are a lot of safety concerns, so I always had to take it off and enter those strength workouts manually, and I never felt it was super accurate. And there’s no haptic motor. I much prefer waking up to my watch vibrating than a blaring sound that could wake my partner if we’re not getting up at the same time. My daily driver is the Oura Ring pretty much 24/7, and then I throw on my Apple Watch when I’m going to the gym, or at night when I want to set an alarm. I ended up making a whole comparison of the two: [Oura Ring vs Apple Watch](/#writing/oura-ring-vs-apple-watch-i-tried-both-for-a-year--a-personal-trainers-take).

## 10. XBloom Studio

[XBloom on Amazon](https://amzn.to/3OWn6Kg)

This is one of my favorite coffee machines, and I’m a huge coffee nerd, but not quite to the point where I’ve committed all the way to espresso and all the gear and practice that come along with it. I’ve had a ton of different setups, from French press to Chemex to V60. I’ve always enjoyed the art of it, and I loved it when I had time to slow down and experiment with different brews and routines. But at the end of the day, what I really wanted was something consistent and easy to use.

A couple of things I love about the XBloom. It’s a three-in-one: a grinder, a brewer and a scale all built in, so you don’t need separate gear. It can be as simple as you want, or as complicated. You can set everything up with a single tap, or you can get into their mobile app and control literally every setting: as many pours as you want, and the temperature, direction and intensity of every pour. What’s great about that is roasters and brewers can dial in their specific beans on the machine. You can buy their XPods, which are freshly roasted, pre-measured beans that ship with an NFC card, so you tap the machine with it and all those settings are dialed in. And you’re not locked into the pods. You can buy their filters and use any beans you want, or set up the buttons in simple mode with presets for light, medium and dark roast, so no matter what beans you buy you get a pretty good cup right off the bat.

One honest pain point is that pod availability and shipping times can be inconsistent, so sometimes you have to plan ahead. I paid around $450, and it came with coffee, filters and everything to get started. If you love coffee, you want something consistent, and you don’t want to fully commit to espresso, I’d highly recommend it.

## Better gear, not more gear

These are the things that have actually stuck: products I reach for almost every single day, that are well built, and that I’d still buy knowing what I know now. The goal for me is not to buy more gear, but to choose better gear with more intention.
