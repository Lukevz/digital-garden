# Documenting Points vs. Maneuvers

When watching Formula 1, what actually gets remembered in the standings are the **points**. First place earns 25, second 18, and so on. It’s a clean summary of who did best on race day.

But anyone who has watched a race knows the points barely scratch the surface. What makes a Grand Prix alive are the **maneuvers**: overtakes, pit calls, tire management, defensive driving, near misses. They create the texture of the race, yet they vanish in the official tally. At the end of the day, you just had to be there to feel the drama.

I’ve been thinking about this as I write and “garden” ideas.
* The **maneuvers** are the raw ingredients of my thinking — the articles I read, notes I scribble, conversations I reference.
* The **points** are the distilled insights I decide to keep: the published note, the idea worth carrying forward.

You can document every maneuver, but it becomes overwhelming and never captures the energy of being “in the race.” Better to preserve the points — the outcomes, the conclusions, the lessons — while still giving credit to the ingredients that inspired them.

In this way, digital gardening isn’t about creating a perfect record of every step in thought. It’s about recording what counts, while remembering that the maneuvers are what made the journey real.
