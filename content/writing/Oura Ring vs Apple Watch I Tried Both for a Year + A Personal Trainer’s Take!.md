# Oura Ring vs Apple Watch: I Tried Both for a Year + A Personal Trainer’s Take!

[Oura Ring vs Apple Watch: I Tried Both for a Year + A Personal Trainer’s Take!](https://youtu.be/NrFJ3an87GY)

A year ago I sold my Apple Watch to go all in on the Oura Ring, but a few months ago I bought an Apple Watch again. This is a breakdown of all the differences to help you decide which one is for you, or, if you’re like me, whether you should get both. You’ll also hear from my wife, Claire, who’s a certified personal trainer and has had both an Oura Ring and an Apple Watch, on which one she recommends and what it’s like to train with each every single day.

Like most tech products these days, there’s not really a wrong answer. It just comes down to the trade-offs you’re willing to live with.

## Passive vs. active

Right up front, an important distinction: one of these is what I’d consider a passive tracker, and the other is a very active tracker. On one hand you have a small smartphone on your wrist that gets notifications, runs different apps, downloads music and podcasts, and does a ton beyond health tracking. There’s a screen, you can interact with it, and you can check things in real time.

The Oura Ring is designed to be unobtrusive. There are no vibrations from notifications and no screen that could distract you, which also means you basically always need the app on your phone to consume data. I think most people can make the decision on that factor alone. The general consensus I’ve seen is that if you like the Apple Watch, you’re willing to put up with the aesthetic (maybe you even prefer it), and you want as many features as possible, get the watch. A lot of people don’t want it to look like they’re always wearing a piece of technology or a personal tracker.

## Why I switched to Oura

That was a big deciding factor about a year ago, because I’d started to build up my own analog collection of watches and wanted to wear them more often. But every time I did, I got more and more gaps in my data, because I’d take my Apple Watch off and forget to charge it or forget to put it back on. I was trying to cut out drinking at the time and wanted to see how it affected my sleep, and if I hadn’t charged the watch or forgot to put it on when I went to bed, I just didn’t get any of that data.

For context, I go to the gym three or four times a week and pretty much walk every day. I’m by no means a fitness enthusiast, but I’m generally tracking some sort of activity almost every day.

Right off the bat I was impressed with how easy it was to get my data and actually understand what it meant. The Apple Health app just isn’t very well designed. I never really know where to look, so I honestly stopped spending time in there and relied on the activity rings, which don’t tell the full story because they’re really only about your calorie and move goals, not your overall stress and where your health is heading.

With Oura I started to learn that it was trying to give me a good representation of the whole day, not just what I felt at that point in time. It tracks your physiological stress and how your body reacts to different events, plus anything you manually input, which they call tags. You can tag drinking, travel, being sick, any number of things, and it uses that to help decide whether it had a positive or negative impact on your health. If you invest the time up front to figure out what works and what doesn’t, you start to notice things. Sometimes I’d wake up feeling like I’d slept great and check Oura to see a 65 or 70 for sleep or readiness. Readiness especially was important, because when it was lower I almost without fail felt it by the afternoon or evening.

There’s a lot of debate about whether checking these scores can dictate how you feel, almost like a placebo effect. I think some of that comes from people who are already checking their data too often and using it as confirmation bias. This is where the ring helps detach you from the numbers, because they’re not always accessible. If you want to check your sleep score or enter something, you need your phone, and it’s a lot harder to get sucked in when there’s no screen on your hand.

## Why I went back to the Apple Watch

The ring worked great for a while. Any time I went to the gym I’d take it off, throw on my cheap Fitbit, and track my strength workouts, because you should definitely not be lifting with any piece of jewelry on your fingers. It’s incredibly dangerous. That meant the ring was out for any of my activity tracking. Oura does track activity, but unless it’s something very basic I don’t feel it’s very accurate. A walk, sure, even runs, and my wife does a lot of dance fitness and says it’s pretty accurate for that. But you can’t wear it for lifting, so you enter it manually, and the data isn’t as accurate. I also feel like the threshold for what counts as an activity is pretty low. Even while I was filming B-roll for this video it triggered a potential walk. You can dismiss it in the app, but it felt like a clunky way to track activity.

The final thing that led me back was wanting a subtle way to wake up in the morning that didn’t wake my partner or wasn’t annoying like most phone alarms. Because the ring has no haptic motor, there was no way to get a subtle vibration, and really my only option was the Apple Watch. I also wanted something that helps with heart rate zones and has a built-in timer I can reference during a workout, and it didn’t hurt that it could replace my need for a phone.

That’s an unexpected benefit, actually. Because the Apple Watch can download podcasts and music, I can fully leave my phone at the apartment when I go to the gym. I go down with a water bottle, AirPods already in, the watch on my wrist tracking my workout and interval times, and basically just carrying my keys. That might not be your reason for getting one, but there are quite a few little quality-of-life features the ring just can’t compete with:

- **Alarm.** Using it as my alarm every morning has been amazing. I still use an app called Sleep Cycle, which lets you set a wake-up window of 30 or 40 minutes, and it tries to detect when you’re most awake. If you want to be up by 7:30, you can set 7:00 to 7:30, and if you’re more awake at 7:15, that’s when it goes off.
- **HomeKit and Siri.** I’m fully on Apple HomeKit, so I can turn a light on right from my wrist. We all know Siri is horrible right now, but every now and then it’s helpful to add something to the groceries list or send a quick text without using my phone.
- **Unlocking my Mac.** With my work laptop I have a pretty long password, and entering it 10 to 15 times a day is a little annoying. Now every time I sit down, it just unlocks.

I’ll call out that I’ve been really strict about notifications on the Apple Watch. I’ve pretty much disabled all of them. It barely vibrates unless it’s a milestone during a workout, like hitting a mile. I completely locked it out from being a mini smartphone, and you do need to be intentional with that, otherwise it’s just another device distracting you.

## Head to head

**Sleep.** Oura tends to be more realistic about my overall day than just how I’m feeling in the moment. You may get some low scores, but I trust it more because it generally represents how I feel throughout the day. The Apple Watch is fairly accurate, but it’s almost always 5, 10, sometimes even 15 percent higher than Oura, which makes me feel like it’s being overly positive. The Watch does get you the wake-up alarm and third-party apps in general, so you get to choose whatever app you want rather than being locked into a big brand.

**Stress and resilience.** These aren’t really vocabulary you’ll find in the Apple Health ecosystem. The Apple Watch pushes you to move, sleep well, work out, and stand up, with reminders and step goals. The Oura Ring looks at your health more holistically. Stress is your *physiological* stress, which people sometimes confuse with their emotional state. Sometimes there’s a correlation, but it’s important to know when your body is physiologically stressed, and that isn’t always something negative. Going to the gym is really stressful for your body and doesn’t mean something bad is happening, but drinking alcohol will also stress your body out and you won’t sleep as well. Resilience is how well your body recovers: if I worked out a ton one night, how well did I sleep, and how do I feel the next morning? The Apple Watch keeps it simple with the three rings (move, stand and exercise), so you have to interpret how well you slept and what impacted it yourself. Because you can add tags on the ring, it’s a lot easier to trace things over time, and that data is shared with the community too. Everybody’s different, but it gives you a lot more info. If you go with just the Apple Watch, there are third-party apps that pair nicely, like Athlytic.

**Workout tracking.** For me this isn’t a close call, but the answer isn’t as cleanly cut for most people. It really comes down to your fitness lifestyle. If you’re doing rock climbing or extreme sports, or marathons (get the Ultra), I’d definitely recommend the Apple Watch. If you’re generally walking and doing light cardio, or activities that wouldn’t be dangerous to do while wearing a ring, you can get away with Oura. Maybe you like using a timer so you don’t need your phone. I use an app called Strong to plan my workouts, so as soon as I finish a set I can trigger the timer on my watch and keep going. You can’t do anything like that with a ring.

**Battery.** This is a big difference, and the clear winner is Oura. You charge it about once a week, maybe twice depending on how often you work out. What works well for me, having both, is to take the ring off and put it on the charger when I head to the gym, and wear the watch during that time. When I get back it’s almost always at 100 percent. Do that twice a week and I almost never run out of battery, and there’s only a 30 to 60 minute gap where the ring isn’t on, which the watch covers because I’m working out. The Apple Watch (I have the Series 11) lasts me about two days, and if you use more apps and interactions than I do, you may have to charge it daily. I kind of compare the Oura Ring to a Kindle and the Apple Watch to an iPad. I charge my iPad Mini once or twice a week, and my Kindle maybe once a month.

**Form factor.** The ring is smaller and a lot more sleek and out of the way. Everyone knows what a smartwatch looks like, and honestly nothing beats an actual analog watch. Even a budget analog watch probably looks better. That comes down to personal preference, but I feel like the Apple Watch looks like you’re wearing a fitness tracker, and the ring is more subtle. I’ll call out that the ring is pretty big, though. I wear it as my wedding ring, so it goes on my ring finger, and I can’t fully close my fingers because it pushes out on the sides. It’s a bigger profile than most rings, so consider that when picking your size and finger. Oura sends a sizing kit of 3D-printed rings so you can wear the plastic one for a while before committing. They felt slightly thicker than the actual metal ring, which is a pretty premium product.

**Software.** With the ring you’re forced to have the app on your phone to get any data, and you have to pay a membership, I believe about $7 a month or $70 a year. Definitely not nothing. That said, I think the Oura app is a lot more premium and easier to navigate than Apple’s. With the Apple Watch you have all the apps you need on your wrist, you don’t pay a membership, and you rarely need to open the phone. Realistically you shouldn’t have to spend that much time in either app. But since you pay for Oura, I personally think it’s worth it. They do a good job of keeping it updated and rolling out new features. Apple isn’t focused on being a fitness tracker, they’re maintaining an entire ecosystem. So the trade-off is a really solid fitness app, or a mediocre fitness app with a ton of other apps along with it.

## Claire’s take

Claire has had her Oura Ring just as long as I have, and she used to have an Apple Watch. She’s a personal trainer with a very different activity profile from mine: a lot of dance fitness, cardio and Pilates, plus some strength training and HIIT.

She has really small wrists, and even the smallest Apple Watch feels clunky, and it cuts into her wrist when she bends it forward and back. She really loves the functionality, timers, music, voice texting, but because it feels so big on her wrist she hasn’t gone back. The Oura, being so much slimmer, is a lot more functional for her. It picks up her dance workouts and cardio, and calorie tracking during those is accurate and passive. When she does lift, she takes the ring off, finishes the session, and logs it manually later, which is a minor inconvenience since strength training is a small part of her overall fitness. For me it’s 80 to 90 percent of what I do, which is exactly why we landed in different places.

She also says the Apple Watch sleep scores run higher than Oura’s, which makes it feel overly positive, and that she prefers sleeping with just the ring. The watch isn’t uncomfortable, but as a bigger device you notice the sensors flashing when you’re winding down, and your finger blocks most of that light with the ring. She likes Oura for stress and recovery, since it tells her when she needs more rest or sleep and how her recovery is actually going, and as someone who has a harder time dialing it back than actually doing something, that’s been really useful. She adds that when tracking a workout on the Apple Watch you have to go in and pick your workout and start it, whereas Oura just picks it up and logs it for you, and you can categorize it later.

One thing she flagged that most reviewers miss: if you’re on a weight loss or weight gain journey, I’d recommend the Apple Watch for one reason. Your body weight shifts, which means your hands shift, and at this time Oura doesn’t offer a free replacement if your ring size changes. And if you like the look of a stack, there are small businesses that make ornate cuffs to go around your Oura ring. ## So which one?

There are three potential options: an Apple Watch, an Oura Ring, or both. I’m going to assume most people don’t want both. It’s a luxury, and kind of overkill, and you can really get away with one or the other.

- **Get the Oura Ring** if you’re not a huge tech person and just want something that tracks your overall health and fitness, you don’t want to be distracted by another device, and you want something reliable and discreet.
- **Get the Apple Watch** if you like the idea of getting a lot more than a fitness tracker, a mini iPod, a phone, a personal assistant, whatever else. That’s your only option, because you have to get a smartwatch to do those features. You also don’t have to buy a $400 watch. It doesn’t have to be an Apple Watch, you could get a Fitbit or another third-party device, especially if you don’t have an iPhone. I specifically got an Apple Watch because it integrates with the ecosystem really well, and I didn’t want to deal with another brand that didn’t integrate well, because that usually meant a lot of manual entry, and that’s what I was doing with the ring anyway. If you have an iPhone and want to save money, the Apple Watch SE 3 is their budget smartwatch and it can do almost anything you need.
- **Get both** if your fitness life is split: you want passive overnight health tracking *and* active in-session workout data, and you’re willing to charge the watch and treat each device as a dedicated tool. The Oura handles sleep and recovery, the Apple Watch handles the gym. They aren’t redundant, they’re complementary, as long as you use each one intentionally.

In conclusion, the perfect health tracker doesn’t really exist, because it’s not just about health tracking. You may also want to track fitness or use it as a tool. At the end of the day it comes down to your personal goals and what your budget can handle.
