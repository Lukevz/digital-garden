## Current role

I'm a senior product designer at Instinct Science (https://instinct.vet/), where I design software for veterinarians. I joined at the end of 2025 after leaving PwC.

## Career timeline

- 2012–2013: YouTube (self-started) — Built a Mac OS app review and tutorial channel that grew to 30k+ subscribers and multi-million views; taught myself writing, video, graphic design, and web from scratch.
- ~2015–2018: Freelance — Solo client work in HTML/CSS, WordPress, graphic design, web design, and video production for local and regional businesses.
- 2018: Founded a creative production company — Formalized the freelance work into an official business.
- 2021: Blue Corona Inc (Senior Web Designer) — Designed and built custom websites for 80+ home service companies; fast-paced client work focused on marketing and delivery speed.
- 2022: PwC Digital, Products & Technology (Senior UX Designer) — Led proof-of-concept product designs; pitched and rebuilt the company's design system in Figma; drove a department-wide migration from Sketch/Abstract/InVision to Figma and upskilled 120+ designers through documentation, office hours, and a self-made curriculum.
- 2023: PwC Digital (UX Manager / Design System Manager) — Promoted to manager; led and mentored a team of three designers; managed the design system that serviced 3,000+ products globally; was lead designer on SDLC Canvas, the firm's flagship AI product; designed POC work for Fortune 500 client pitches.
- End of 2025: Joined Instinct Science as Senior Product Designer.

## Why I left PwC for Instinct

PwC was like a crazy academy — it threw me into as many different situations as possible, and I'll forever be grateful for the experience, the network, and the connections. But it's very fast-paced, and for better or worse you can start to feel like one more cog in the machine. I prefer being close to the published version of my work, not just pouring in daily effort and never really knowing how it landed or what the impact was.

I love the freedom of a smaller company, and I love that the work is directly tied to genuinely helping people with daily problems. Veterinarians are overworked, understaffed, under-resourced, and stressed. If I can play a part in reducing that stress and making their lives a little happier, that drives a lot of my design decisions and a lot of my passion for being at Instinct.

## How I think about design

See `/content/about/philosophy.md` for the full version. Short version:

Design is pattern recognition — but the patterns that matter most come from the physical world, not other apps. I always define the baseline first (what are people doing today?), ask whether the problem even needs to be solved before touching any tools, and think in systems that will scale. Simple doesn't mean basic — it means the most elegant form of complexity.

My specialty is design systems because they force you to think at the system level. A design system isn't a component library — it's a shared language. The components are the output. The real work is the decisions, the documentation, and getting people to actually use it.
