Good design is an answer to a series of questions, and most of my process is about asking them before touching any tools. I start every problem with [[Define the baseline first]] and the [[Six questions before touching any tools]], because the bar I'm aiming for is [[Simple is the most elegant form of complexity]]: distilled, not dumbed down. The belief underneath all of it is that [[The best patterns come from the physical world not other apps]]. Design is pattern recognition, but copying other software is just templating, and I think a lot of designers confuse the two.

On working style, I push to [[Remove layers between you and the user]] because every intermediary dilutes your read on the actual person you're designing for, and I try to [[Do not get attached to your work|not get attached to the work]] since throwing things away is part of the job. The career-shaping version of that mindset is [[Think like a business owner not an order-taker]]. My taste runs minimal and clean, but [[I have a soft spot for skeuomorphism]] and I'll defend Apple's boldness with things like liquid glass.

On AI: [[AI amplifies your process it does not fix it]], and the second-order effect I care about most is that [[AI makes taste the differentiator]]. The people who shaped this thinking are in [[Designers and thinkers I come back to]], and the systems end of it lives at [[Design Systems MOC]]. Who I am behind all this: [[I am wired for pattern recognition]].
