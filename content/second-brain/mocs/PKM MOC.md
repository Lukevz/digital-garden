My whole approach to personal knowledge management starts from the belief that [[A digital garden is a philosophy not a website]]. It's thought-based instead of project-based, it lowers the barrier to sharing, and as a neurodivergent it lets my brain follow what catches its eye without falling into consume-only paralysis. What drove me to it was that [[Social media turned self-expression into self-promotion]]. The public face of it is [[lukevz.com is hand-built with vanilla HTML CSS and JS|lukevz.com]], mapped section by section at [[Site MOC]], and the private capture layer is Bear; see [[Figma and Bear are my daily tools]].

Two rules do most of the work. [[Points are worth documenting maneuvers are not]] decides what's worth keeping: the distilled insight, not every article and scribble behind it. And [[File over app is why I keep everything in markdown]] decides the format, which is why switching from a Bear-and-Blot pipeline to a hand-built site changed nothing about the notes themselves. On the task side, [[A todo app should be easy to capture unopinionated and clear your mind]] and [[Things 3 is the todo app I settled on]] cover where the doing goes, as opposed to the thinking.

The newest thread is [[52 is about owning your thinking again|52]], my local markdown note-taking app, which comes from the same conviction as [[AI makes taste the differentiator]]: never outsource your thinking. The books that shaped how I think about creative work and trust are at [[Creativity Inc and The Speed of Trust are my two book recommendations]]. The daily practice that feeds all of this is at [[My daily routine runs 9 to 5 from home]], and the morning writing habit that finally stuck is at [[Morning pages finally made journaling stick]]. Once a year the whole thing gets reviewed in public; see [[I do an annual review built on a yearly theme]].
