Design systems are my specialty, and my core take is that [[Design systems are a shared language not a component library]]. The components are the output; the real work is the decisions, the documentation, and adoption. I like design systems because they force you to think at the system level, which is where my head already lives; see [[I am wired for pattern recognition]].

I didn't choose them so much as get forced into them: [[I fell into design systems by drowning in website volume]] at Blue Corona, the marketing agency where a reusable grayscale block system took sites from ten-plus hours down to under two. That experience became the pitch at PwC, the consulting firm where I spent 2022 through 2025: [[I rebuilt PwC's design system in Figma to prove the switch]] largely in my free time, then [[I pitched and led PwC's migration to Figma]] across 120-plus designers, then [[I ran the design system serving 3000 products at PwC]] as manager. What that rollout actually taught me is that [[Rolling out a design system is a teaching job]]: office hours and 40 pages of documentation moved adoption more than any component did. The scar tissue is real too: [[Figma variables v1 caused more harm than help at scale]] cost us months of cleanup and taught me not to bet a live library on a v1 platform feature.

Where I've landed after all of it: [[Design tokens are the unlock]], for theming and now for how AI agents consume design, and [[Most companies do not need a custom design system]]. PwC justified custom; most teams should theme a popular base instead. Today the systems thinking continues at Instinct, where [[I design software for veterinarians at Instinct]]. The broader philosophy is at [[Design MOC]], and the career arc is at [[Career MOC]].
