In early 2022 PwC was still running on Sketch, Abstract, and InVision. Fresh off the reusable-components approach from [[I fell into design systems by drowning in website volume|my agency years]], I proposed doing the same thing in Figma, and then I proved it the hard way: I led rebuilding the entire design system, the fourth iteration at the time, in Figma, taking on 80-plus percent of the work myself. Months of it happened in my free time on top of actual product work.

It was very manual. Figma could import Sketch files, but layers, components, and styling all needed rework. Funny detail: tokens didn't even exist in Figma yet, you only had styles and themes you could publish in a library. The fourth version was more robust, with multiple color themes, so a clean-cut rebuild in Figma made far more sense than dragging it forward in Sketch.

The rebuild was good enough to demo how much faster Figma was, even just throwing things together in a shared canvas. That demo convinced leadership to invest, and we ran a trial with a few pilot projects. The department-wide rollout that followed is its own story: [[I pitched and led PwC's migration to Figma]], and what it taught me about adoption is [[Rolling out a design system is a teaching job]].
