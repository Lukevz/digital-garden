Good design is an answer to a series of questions. Before anything else you need a crystal-clear understanding of the problem, and that's harder than it sounds. The more you try to define it, the more complex it feels. It's an uphill battle to figure out what the problem actually is, then to keep iterating until you can deliver the simplest version of a solution.

The six questions I always ask at the start:

- What problem are we solving?
- Who are we solving it for?
- Why are we solving it?
- Does it even need to be solved?
- Does it need to be solved now?
- Does it need to be solved by us?

I run these before touching any tools, right alongside [[Define the baseline first]]. The bar for the answer is [[Simple is the most elegant form of complexity]]. This is the core of my process at [[Design MOC]].
