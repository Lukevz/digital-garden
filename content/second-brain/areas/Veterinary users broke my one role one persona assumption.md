The hardest part of designing veterinary software, and what shocked me most about it, is how much context switching and detail veterinarians hold while making genuinely hard calls in stressful situations. I knew being a vet was tough, but I had the happier notion that they mostly get to work with animals. Really they're seeing the heavy side of it, especially in the emergency clinics Instinct serves. That reframed a lot of my [[I design software for veterinarians at Instinct|work at Instinct]].

## One role can need three or four personas

The bigger surprise was that a single job title covers wildly different people. I was used to typecasting my users. At PwC I'd design for a CEO, a product manager, and an engineer, and those were clean, clear-cut roles. In veterinary medicine you can have three or four different types of doctors, CSRs (client service representatives), or vet techs under the same title, largely driven by the size of the practice and whether it's general practice or ER (emergency). So I almost need multiple personas for the same role. It's probably true in other industries too, but veterinary work is where it broke my old habit and forced me to run the [[Six questions before touching any tools]] harder.
