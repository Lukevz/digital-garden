I fell into design systems around the summer of 2021, not by choice but by volume. I was a senior web designer at Blue Corona, a marketing agency, one of two seniors plus a few juniors, and we were responsible for a huge number of home-services website projects at once. There was no set size to a project: some sites ran hundreds of pages off a handful of custom templates. We had to start building reusable blocks or drown.

## The grayscale page generator

Figma was just getting popular and I helped move the team off Photoshop onto it. Then I helped build what was basically a web page generator: grayscale variations of every core marketing block, heroes, calls to action, forms, about sections, products and services, coupons, that you could drag into a file and swap out. That grew into a full system of buttons, icons, and hero lockups. Sites that used to take ten-plus hours of design time dropped to under two. I wrote about that in an old case study on automating workflows.

I was learning from Figma's free YouTube resources and Brad Frost's atomic design approach at the time. That agency grind is why I believe [[Design systems are a shared language not a component library]]: the components only mattered because the whole team used them the same way. It set up everything that came next at PwC; see [[I rebuilt PwC's design system in Figma to prove the switch]]. Hub: [[Design Systems MOC]].
