A critique is an exchange, a back-and-forth, meant to be very collaborative and conversational. It's not a presentation. As the designer you do want to walk through the work and set the stage for the conversation, but that's the setup, not the point. If you're the only one talking, it isn't a critique.

The way I run one is to include as many different personas as possible. It doesn't always have to be super cross-functional, you can have just a bunch of designers together, but I do think the more departments or personas you have the better: engineering and product, maybe QA, any relevant leadership. You get as many perspectives as possible that way.

The other thing I'm deliberate about is who's in the room. I always try to include people who will almost always provide feedback, or at least say something on every call. You should expect, and put together, a group that is going to actively pick apart or dive into the subject matter. That's the same instinct as [[Remove layers between you and the user]]: more direct signal, fewer people nodding along. It also pairs with [[Do not get attached to your work]], because a critique only works if getting picked apart is the expected outcome rather than a bad day. Hub: [[Design MOC]].
