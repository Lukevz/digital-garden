Design is mostly systematic pattern recognition, but the patterns that matter most aren't from other software. They're from the physical world. How do people know to push versus pull a door? How do they know where to park? Those patterns feel completely natural because they've been adopted at mass scale, and the best digital UX borrows from them, not from what another app does.

## Copying apps is templating, not pattern recognition

A lot of designers copy other software thinking they're doing pattern recognition. They're not. They're templating. Real pattern recognition means finding what's already familiar and natural to people in everyday life, then adapting it.

The caveat: people are complex and different. Some metaphors land for some users and completely miss others. Constantly reusing digital heuristics that work in some contexts won't always translate. You have to stay attuned to why something feels natural, not just that it does. This is the belief I'd defend hardest at [[Design MOC]], and it's the same instinct behind [[Simple is the most elegant form of complexity]].
