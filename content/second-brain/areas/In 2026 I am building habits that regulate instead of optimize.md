My focus for 2026 is a calmer mind and a regulated environment, not a more optimized one. Less doomscrolling and more bloomscrolling. Less jumping between activities and multitasking, more staying in the moment. One device at a time, one habit at a time. It's the direct lesson from the year I wrote up in [[I do an annual review built on a yearly theme]]: your nervous system matters more than your productivity. There are seven habits I've been cultivating, and the full write-up is at /#writing/7-habits-routines-that-regulate-and-bring-me-peace-in-2026.

## The seven habits

Morning pages, every weekday before work, covered in [[Morning pages finally made journaling stick]]. Moving every day, averaging 7,500 steps on top of lifting three times a week, because [[My daily routine runs 9 to 5 from home|working from home]] makes it far too easy to sit through a whole day. Scrapbooking with my wife, which is the analog one. Cleaning as I go instead of letting it pile up for the weekend. Photo walks, with a goal of at least ten this year. Getting social media off my phone. And a nightly turndown.

## Why the analog ones matter most

Scrapbooking and photo walks are doing the heaviest lifting and I didn't expect that. We printed travel photos, bought a scrapbook, and spent almost three hours at the kitchen table collaging. As a digital artist there's something genuinely refreshing about having to commit to a design with no undo. It's memory-tending, and it made me feel like a kid again. Photo walks work the same way: the rule is I bring the camera and I take photos, and the mindfulness is the real output, see [[Photography scratches the same itch as design]] and [[I shoot Fujifilm]].

## The nightly turndown

My wife and I stayed at an all-inclusive resort and realized the most calming thing about it wasn't the buffet, it was the nightly turndown. So I run one at home: blackout curtains closed, covers folded at the top, a 6oz glass of water filled, lavender spray on the pillows, and rain sounds from the Calm app. It sounds boujie. I've kept it up for four months, and it's the physical cue that tells my body to start winding down. Hubs: [[Life MOC]], [[Site MOC]].
