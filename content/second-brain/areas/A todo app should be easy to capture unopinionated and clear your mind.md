I judge every todo app on three things, in this order: it has to be easy to capture into, it has to be unopinionated, and it has to actually clear your mind. Everything else is decoration.

Easy to capture means I can get a task in from my phone or my desk without thinking about where it goes, at whatever level of detail I happen to have. Sometimes that's four words, sometimes it's a full breakdown with notes. If capture has friction, the app is already dead, because the tasks just never make it in.

Unopinionated means the app doesn't force its methodology on me. A lot of productivity tools are really someone else's system with a UI on top, and the moment my week doesn't match their model I'm fighting the tool. And clearing your mind is the whole point: the reason to write it down is to stop carrying it, so if I still feel the weight of my list after using the app, it failed at the only job that matters.

Picking a todo app is like picking a car. You want something reliable to get you from A to B, and all the bells and whistles are irrelevant if your actual usage is light. This is only about personal productivity; team work happens in whatever the team uses, Asana, Notion, Trello, or Todoist.
